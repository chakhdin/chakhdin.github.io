The Sky-Watcher NEQ6 Pro is a computerised German equatorial mount with SynScan GoTo control. It carries all of my Italian setups: the deep-sky refractor and the 8-inch ACF, the planetary train and the solar refractor.

Mine has the Rowan Astronomy belt modification, which replaces the factory spur-gear drive with timing belts to reduce backlash and periodic error. The mount was then tuned by Davide Dal Prato of the Torre del Sole astronomical park in Brembate di Sopra (BG), Italy.

I bought it second-hand, as my second purchase when I started taking astrophotography seriously. On Subito, the Italian classifieds site, I found a seller offering a Sky-Watcher 120 together with the mount; I asked to buy the mount alone and drove there to see and try it, a nice trip that my wife came on too.

About 10 to 12 months later I had the belts fitted and the mount tuned, because it was clearly old: by my estimate more than ten or twelve years. Guiding improved considerably: the total RMS is now almost always below 0.8″, and on the better nights 0.5″, which is what I like to get from this mount, with both the [FSQ85-EDX](/equipment/takahashi-fsq85-edx/) and the [Meade 8" ACF](/equipment/meade-8-acf/). The Meade is the heaviest configuration; the rest of the train is the same on both telescopes, with the filter wheel, the off-axis guider and the camera.

I drive the mount with EQMOD. For polar alignment I use SharpCap when Polaris is visible, otherwise N.I.N.A.'s Three Point Polar Alignment plugin. For transport, the mount travels in a Geoptik bag and the tripod in a second bag.
