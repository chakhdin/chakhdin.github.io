title: "ASI2600MM Pro"
description: "ZWO ASI2600MM Pro: my main deep-sky camera, a cooled monochrome APS-C camera."
---
The ZWO ASI2600MM Pro is a cooled monochrome camera built around the Sony IMX571 back-illuminated APS-C sensor: 23.5 × 15.7 mm, 6248 × 4176 pixels (26 MP) of 3.76 µm, with a 16-bit ADC.

It is my main deep-sky camera, used on two setups that share the same imaging train: the [Takahashi FSQ85-EDX](/equipment/takahashi-fsq85-edx/) and the [Meade 8" ACF](/equipment/meade-8-acf/), both on the [NEQ6 Pro](/equipment/neq6-pro/). The train:

- ZWO EFW 7 × 36 mm filter wheel with [Antlia LRGB-V Pro](/equipment/antlia-lrgb-v-pro-36mm-filters/) and [Antlia 3 nm Pro SHO](/equipment/antlia-pro-3nm-sho-36mm-filters/) filters
- ZWO off-axis guider with a ZWO ASI290MM Mini guide camera (Sony IMX290, 2.9 µm pixels)
- Pegasus Astro Pocket Powerbox Advance Gen2 for power, dew heaters and USB

Earlier images with this camera used [Baader LRGB](/equipment/baader-lrgb-36mm-filters/) and [Baader Ha 7 nm](/equipment/baader-ha-7nm-36mm-filter/) 36 mm filters.

Moving to a monochrome camera had always been my dream, but the jump looked too expensive for me: besides the camera I would need a filter wheel and filters. Then a lucky combination came along: a seller was offering a 7 × 36 mm filter wheel, the size I had already chosen for this camera, with Baader LRGB and H-alpha filters, at a very good price. I bought those first, and when I later found the camera at a good price as well, I went ahead. Another chance on AstroSell then brought me the Antlia filters, which are very good.

It changed the way I work a great deal. Acquisition takes longer, because every filter needs its own frames, and there are more images to process. They also take more disk space: the sensor is larger and has more pixels than the [ASI294MC Pro](/equipment/asi294mc-pro/)'s, so every frame is bigger, and each filter needs more of them. Both acquisition and processing have become more complex.

The difference shows above all in the luminance, which brings out more of the fine detail in galaxies and nebulae, and in the narrowband filters for hydrogen-alpha, oxygen III and sulphur.

I use gain 100 and offset 80, for historical reasons, and normally cool the sensor to −10 °C, the same for narrowband and broadband. For calibration I take darks, flats and dark flats; with CMOS cameras I do not use bias frames. I take the flats with a panel: a large 23 cm one for the Meade and a smaller 150 mm one for the FSQ. I keep a dark library and renew it once a year.

Compared with the ASI294MC Pro the sensor is much cleaner: there is no amp glow and the noise is really low. I have never had disconnections or tilt with this camera. I usually shoot luminance, red, green and blue in a 3:1:1:1 ratio; in narrowband I collect as much as I can, with no fixed rule relative to RGB.

A second ASI2600MM Pro, belonging to the rig, works at Swan Reach, South Australia, on the [RCOS 16"](/equipment/rcos-16/) and the [Paramount ME](/equipment/paramount-me/) of the [Swan Reach remote rig](/rigs/swan-reach/). Its train: a ZWO EFW 7 × 2" filter wheel with [Astrodon LRGBSHO 2" filters](/equipment/astrodon-lrgbsho-2-filters/), an off-axis guider with a ZWO ASI174MM Mini guide camera, and a Pegasus Astro Pocket Powerbox Advance Gen2.
