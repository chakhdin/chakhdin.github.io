This capture used a very different instrument from the rest of the collection: the 162mm refractor of the Gruppo Astrofili Rozzano (GAR), from around 1870, with a G.&S. Merz objective from Munich on a J.H. Dallmeyer mount from London, housed at the Rozzano civic observatory and paired with a 2x Barlow lens. The long, slow optical path and 19th-century glass render Jupiter's belts with a distinct soft, warm character quite unlike the modern views elsewhere in this gallery, though the same equatorial banding and pale hollow near the South Equatorial Belt are still clearly resolved.
