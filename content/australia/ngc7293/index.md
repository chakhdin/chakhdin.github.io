NGC 7293, the Helix Nebula, is a planetary nebula in the constellation Aquarius and is listed as Caldwell 63. It is one of the closest planetary nebulae to the Sun: the Gaia parallax gives about 655 light-years, while ESO and ESA quote about 700. Its apparent diameter is about 25 arcminutes, and its main ring is about two light-years across. Karl Ludwig Harding discovered it, probably before 1824.

The nebula is the outer layers of a Sun-like star, cast off at the end of its life. The ultraviolet light of the hot white dwarf left at the centre makes the gas glow: oxygen (OIII) in the blue-green inner disc, hydrogen (H-alpha) in the red outer ring. Along the inner edge of the ring are the cometary knots, dense clumps of gas with tails pointing away from the central star, each about as large as the Solar System. Estimates of their number range from about 20,000 to 40,000.

The data was captured remotely from Swan Reach, South Australia, over six nights, in collaboration with Paride Gabini, Rino Morra, Federico Palaia and Alessio Carmine Scipione, on a rig operated with NINA at Swan Reach Imaging (https://swanreachimaging.com.au).
