NGC 300 is a spiral galaxy in the constellation Sculptor, about 6 million light-years away. It is seen nearly face-on, with loose, flocculent arms rather than a sharply defined spiral pattern, and it is one of the closest and brightest members of the Sculptor Group.

Blue knots of young stars and pink star-forming regions are scattered along the arms, while the surface brightness of the disc is low, which is why a long luminance integration was needed to bring out the outer regions.

The data was captured remotely from Swan Reach, South Australia, over seven nights, on a rig operated with NINA at Swan Reach Imaging (https://swanreachimaging.com.au), and was collected jointly by the group sharing the rig. Stacked and processed in PixInsight.
