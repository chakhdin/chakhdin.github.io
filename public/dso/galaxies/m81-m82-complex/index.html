M81, M82 and surroundings

"A wide-field capture of Bode's Galaxy (M81) and the Cigar Galaxy (M82), framed by the delicate Integrated Flux Nebula (IFN)."

This wide-field capture highlights the dynamic grouping of Bode’s Galaxy (M81) and the Cigar Galaxy (M82), framed by the delicate, wispy structures of the Integrated Flux Nebula (IFN).

Dominating the center of the frame is M81, a grand-design spiral galaxy presenting a brilliantly warm core and sweeping spiral arms punctuated by pink star-forming regions. Positioned above it is the edge-on M82. The intense gravitational tidal forces from its larger neighbor have ignited massive starburst activity within M82, visible here as striking red plumes of ionized hydrogen gas erupting outward from its galactic plane perpendicular to the disk.

Filling the vast spaces between and around these distant galaxies is the Integrated Flux Nebula. These faint, ghostly clouds of dust and gas reside much closer to home, sitting high above the galactic plane of our own Milky Way. Unlike traditional emission or reflection nebulae, the IFN is weakly illuminated by the combined faint glow of all the stars in the Milky Way rather than a single proximate light source. Bringing these faint tendrils out of the background darkness requires deep integration times and careful processing to preserve the bright galactic cores. Scattered throughout the surrounding field are numerous smaller, distant background galaxies.
